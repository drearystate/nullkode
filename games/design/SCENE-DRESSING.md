# Putting a scene together

Reference for dressing 3D adventure screens (the kit's set dresser, and any person or AI placing assets by hand).
Studied from six target images (a ruined gate in a forest, a top-down ruin arena, a cliff side-scroller, a
village from above, a village street, a sunset ruins duel) and published biome showcase shots. The models in those images
are no better than ours; the difference is where things are put, how they are lit and how they meet each other.

## 1. Every screen has a job and a hero
- **One focal point per screen**, and everything leads to it: the glowing archway, the rune circle, the treasure
  alcove, the fountain, the portal. It is the brightest, warmest and most detailed thing on screen.
- **Leading lines** carry the eye there: a path that bends toward it, a row of bollards or lanterns, a stream, a
  trail of coins. Paths are S-curves, never straight corridors.
- **Second and third focal points sit further back** (fountain → windmill → castle on the hill), so the screen has
  depth and promises places to go.
- **Gameplay is a focal point too**: the fight space is an open, readable floor; the goal is lit; a reward is in a
  warm pool of light on a little raised step.

## 1b. A playable screen is closed, with few ways out
A showpiece shot can be open; a game screen cannot. Every screen is enclosed on all sides by something that reads as
impassable at a glance, and the player leaves only by 1–3 clear exits.
- **Barriers by place:** dungeons and ruins = full-height walls (broken tops are fine, gaps are not); forest = a solid
  tree line two or three trees deep with bushes filling the trunks; hills = cliffs and rock shelves; water = a river or
  lake too wide to cross; village = house fronts joined by walls, fences, hedges and the river.
- **Nothing ambiguous:** a low wall, a lone rock or a gap between trees reads as "maybe I can go there". If the player
  may not pass, it must look solid and tall enough; if they may, it must look like a way through.
- **Exits are dressed and lit:** a path that runs off the edge, a gate, an arch, stairs, a bridge, a cave mouth. The
  camera can see past the barrier (canopy, cliffs, distant ruins), but the player cannot walk there.
- **Blocked-for-now exits look different from walls:** a boulder, thorns, a locked gate, deep water, so the player
  remembers to come back.

## 2. Three layers, always
| Layer | What goes there | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| Frame (nearest the camera) | overhanging branches, big leaves, a wall corner, a rail | dark, saturated, partly off-screen; never covers the hero (fade it when the hero is under it) |
| Play (the walkable screen) | floor, props, people, foes | full light, full detail, strongest contrast |
| Beyond (outside the room) | canopy, cliffs, waterfall, distant ruins, mountains | lighter, bluer, less saturated, hazy; silhouettes |
A top-down room is not a box floating in the void: you see the world past its walls (trees crowding the corners,
water running outside, a bridge leading out).

## 3. Light does half the work
- **Warm key, cool fill.** Low sun from one side (late afternoon/golden hour by default), long soft shadows, blue
  shade. Rim light on edges.
- **Pools of warm local light on cool surfaces**: braziers, torches, lanterns, lit windows, the glow from a doorway.
  2–6 per screen, placed in pairs around what matters.
- **Light shafts** through canopy or gaps in a ruin, with dust motes in them.
- **Atmospheric depth**: distance fades toward the sky colour.
- **One accent colour against the palette**: red or blue banners on grey stone and green leaves. Banners colour-code
  areas (blue = the hall, red = the treasure alcove).
- Time of day per place and per story beat (sunset ruins, night crypt, bright meadow).
- **Colour that pops but stays warm:** neutral tone mapping (ACES "filmic" greys colours out), exposure about 1.0; a
  strong warm sky fill so shade keeps its colour (shade goes warm-purple, never black); fog only far away. A light
  grade after: a little vibrance, a gentle contrast curve, golden highlights, acid greens toned toward leaf green.
  Push it too far and it goes neon or orange: neutrals (stone, plaster, paths) must stay cream and grey.
- **Repaint models to one palette** by material name: slate-blue roofs, cream plaster, warm brown wood, warm grey
  stone, and windows lit warm from inside. Lit windows do more for "inviting" than any other single change.

## 4. Nothing is clean, nothing is new
- **Moss on every top surface** of stone and rock, thicker on the shady side; **moss drips over ledges**.
- **Vines hang** from walls, arches, cliff faces and tree branches.
- **Grass and small plants grow in joints**: between flagstones, at wall bases, around rock bottoms, on stair edges.
- **Floors vary**: tiles of different sizes, some darker or mossy, some cracked or missing; paths have broken edges
  with grass creeping in.
- **Walls are broken**: uneven heights, missing blocks, collapsed sections, arches standing alone.

## 5. Props come in families, at the edges
- **Clusters of 2–3**: a barrel with two crates, a crate stack with a sack, never one lonely prop and never a grid.
- **Edges, corners and doorways** carry the dressing; the middle of a play space stays open.
- **Pairs flank entrances** (braziers, banners, crates either side of a gate), mirrored but not identical.
- **Big–medium–small**: a large rock with two smaller rocks and pebbles; a tree with a bush and grass at its foot.
- **Flowers in drifts** of one colour, a few as highlights near the camera; white flowers lift dark corners.

## 6. Water, height and edges
- **The ground has levels**: terraces, cliff steps, raised daises, stairs. Even a flat room has a step up to the
  reward.
- **Water always moves and always has edges**: rocks along the bank, foam where it falls, lily pads in still
  pools, small cascades between levels, a waterfall in the distance.
- **Crossings are places**: stone bridges with lanterns and banners, plank bridges, stepping stones.
- **Every exit is dressed**: a path, a bridge, an arch, stairs.

## 7. Settlements
- **Organic plan**: a plaza (fountain, well, market) with curved streets leading out; buildings face the plaza and
  streets, turned slightly, never on a grid.
- **Every building is dressed**: flower boxes, a lantern by the door, chimney smoke, steps, a barrel or bench by the
  wall, a sign for shops (mug for the tavern).
- **Zones**: market stalls in a cluster with produce and crates; tavern with outdoor tables; farm edge (windmill,
  crops, sheep, hay, fences) as the town gives way to country; river with a bridge.
- **Shared palette**: one roof colour, one wall colour, timber, then bright accents (awnings, flowers, bunting).
- **People in small groups doing something**: two chatting, a merchant gesturing, someone carrying a basket, drinkers
  at a table. Never spread evenly, never all idle.
- Trees and hedges between buildings soften the town; lamps line the main way.

## 8. Life
Chimney smoke, falling water, foam, flickering fire, birds, butterflies, falling leaves, dust in light shafts,
fireflies at dusk, sheep, chickens. Every screen has at least two moving things that are not enemies.

## 9. Rules the set dresser follows (top-down camera)
1. Pick the screen's focal point from its contents (exit, chest, shrine, NPC, puzzle, boss door) and light it.
2. Keep the central walkable area clear (no props closer than one tile to a path or the room centre unless they are
   cover by design).
3. Walls and room edges: one prop cluster every 3–4 tiles; pairs either side of every doorway.
4. Ground: base colour + floor variation; plants in joints along walls; moss on every top surface (material rule,
   not per object).
5. Outside the room: fill the margins with canopy, rocks and water so no screen edge shows void.
6. Frame: put 1–2 overhanging canopy pieces on the camera-near edge; fade them over the hero.
7. Lights: 2–6 warm pools in pairs; one light shaft; sky/haze colour per biome and time.
8. Life: ≥ 2 ambient movers.
9. Check the shot: the focal point is the brightest area; the hero and foes stand out against the floor; nothing
   blocks a path; no bare patch larger than 3×3 tiles.

## 10. What we have for it
- KayKit dungeon pack: walls (window, arch, doorway, half, broken), pillars, columns, stairs, torches, banners
  (patterns, shield), barrels, crates, chests (gold, large), tables, shelves, candles, rubble, rocks, weeds tiles.
- KayKit forest nature pack (8 colourways = biomes): 23 trees, bare trees, 22 bushes, 16 grass clumps, ~65 rocks,
  hills and cliffs.
- KayKit medieval hexagon pack: buildings (tavern, smithy, mill, market, shrine, tower, docks, stables, church),
  banners, barrels, boats.
- Kenney fantasy town kit: fountains, windmill, watermill, stalls, carts, lanterns, hedges, fences, roads, bridges.
- Built in code where no model exists: moss (top-facing material blend), vines (hanging strips), light shafts, haze,
  water foam, smoke and other particles.

## 11. The set dresser in the kit (kit 1.5, `engine/src/kit-3d/dress.js`)
A game loads one prop pack (`NK3D.props.add(buffer)` / `NK3D.props.load(url)`; Ember Hollow's is
`work/dress/props.glb`, 65 KayKit models, 473 KB, built by `work/dress/pack-props.mjs` from `props.list`). From then
on `NK3D.rooms` dresses every room by these rules; without a pack, rooms keep the old code-built pieces.
- **§1b enclosure:** where two overworld screens share a wide open edge, one gap three tiles wide stays open on the
  path and the rest becomes border on both sides (`enclose: false` turns it off). The overworld's forest border
  letter "T" is read as border "F" (indoors "T" is a tool chest).
- **Barriers by biome:** forest/marsh = trees with bushes between the trunks; hills/lakeside/desert = mossy rock
  shelves with a few trees. Heights follow the camera: tall on the far edge and beyond, medium on the sides, short
  copses inside the screen, a low hedge or low rocks on the near edge. Ring one tile outside = the neighbour's own
  edge, so screens join up; two more rings only where the world ends.
- **Indoors (themes per dungeon, `rooms({ themes })`: stone · burrow · frost · crypt):** model floor slabs with
  worn small tiles near walls, dirt in burrows; full-height walls (cracked/broken tops), low wall on the near side;
  torches on the back wall, banners in a pair, vines down the back wall, rubble and plants at wall feet; prop
  families (2–3) in corners and along the back wall, never by a door, on the path or in a puzzle room (they get a
  collider).
- **Moss shader** on every upward stone face (frost in the Frostvault, ash in the crypt).
- **Water (ponds):** one smooth surface per pond (a blur of its cells, so the outline is round and organic, never a
  chain of circles): shallow turquoise at the rim to deep teal in the middle, see-through over the muddy bed in the
  shallows, a thin broken foam line at the edge and drifting glints; rounded mossy rocks big-medium-small on the bank,
  reeds and lily pads. Palette override `pal.water = {shallow, deep, foam}`.
- **Light:** warm key and cool fill outdoors, dim cool fill indoors; four pooled point lights per room, focal
  first: braziers flank a dungeon door and the boss door, a glow on every pickup, torches flank locked doors.
- **Frame and air:** two dark crowns past the near corners that fade when the hero walks under them; light shafts
  with dust in forest and marsh.
- **Not automatic yet:** settlements (§7) and townsfolk, bridges and river banks beyond ponds, cliffs and levels
  (§6), a focal light on exits.
- Checks: `work/adventure3d/reach_test.mjs` (every overworld place reachable) and `room_reach.mjs` (every exit
  and pickup in every dungeon room reachable around the props).
